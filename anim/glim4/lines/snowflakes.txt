Why does every snowflake have six sides?
It starts with water molecules, which link up in a hexagon pattern when they freeze.
A tiny ice crystal grows as a six sided prism, high up in the cold clouds.
Its corners poke out into the moist air, so they grow fastest and become six arms.
All six arms float through the same cold and humidity, so they grow almost alike.
And no two snowflakes are the same, because each one takes its own path! Have you seen snow yet?
